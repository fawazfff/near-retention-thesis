A simple question with on-chain data

Does NEAR keep its users?

When an account makes its first transaction, does it come back four weeks later? We counted accounts that signed transactions and followed them week by week.

The short answer: we cannot yet say retention is improving.
Study window: 40 weekly groups · 2 February to 2 November 2026 · NEAR mainnet
ⓘ
One thing to know before reading the numbers

Five weeks have far more new accounts than the others. We have not explained why. Those unusually large groups affect the results, so treat this as an early finding, not a final investment signal.

THE RESULT IN PLAIN ENGLISH
8 in 1,000

In the latest sample, about 8 out of every 1,000 accounts signed another transaction four weeks after their first week.

NO CLEAR CHANGE

We saw a rise. We cannot be sure it is real.

The latest groups had a higher average than the earliest groups. But the uncertainty range runs from a small fall to a rise. The evidence is not strong enough to call it an improvement.

05 / HOW WE DID IT

What do these words mean?

You can read the short version here. Open a section if you want the exact rule used in the research.

What is an account?

A NEAR address that can sign transactions. One person may have more than one account.

What is a cohort?

A group of accounts first seen in the same week. Think of a class of students who all joined on the same Monday.

What does “came back” mean?

The account signed at least one transaction in the fourth week after joining.

What is a percentage point?

If a rate moves from 2% to 3%, it rose by 1 percentage point.

How did we decide an account was new?

We looked for its first signed transaction in the study window. We also checked the 12 weeks before that window. This helps avoid counting accounts that were already active just before the study began. A long-dormant account can still look new under this rule.

How did we measure activity?

An account counts as active in a week if it signed at least one transaction during that Monday-to-Sunday UTC week. Receiving tokens alone does not count.

What is the 95% uncertainty range?

It is a range of values that fit the data under the statistical method we used. For the change between early and late groups, the range is −0.92 to +3.51 percentage points. Because it includes zero, the data cannot rule out no change.

What is a Welch test?

It compares the average of two sets of numbers while allowing the groups to have different amounts of variation. Here, each weekly group contributes one retention rate.

Why remove accounts with many transactions?

Very busy accounts can distort the picture. We repeated the calculation with different cut-offs to see whether the result depended on them. This is only a rough check. It does not identify bots or prove that an account is genuine.

Where did the data come from?

We used the public NEAR mainnet transactions table in Google BigQuery: bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions. The study uses the signer account and transaction date fields.
